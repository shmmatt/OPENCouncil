Status of the Whittier Covered Bridge in Ossipee
Executive Summary
The historic Whittier Covered Bridge in Ossipee is currently open and operational. The bridge is owned and maintained by the New Hampshire Department of Transportation (NHDOT), not the Town of Ossipee. The structure standing today was rebuilt in 1994 after the original bridge was destroyed by arson in 1991.

Clarification of Provided Evidence
The news articles provided for this analysis concern the Saco River Covered Bridge in Conway, which was recently damaged by a vehicle [1]. This is a separate and distinct structure from the Whittier Covered Bridge in Ossipee. The Whittier Bridge spans the Bearcamp River, while the Saco River Bridge is located approximately 20 miles north in a different municipality. Information regarding the Conway bridge is not applicable to the status of the Ossipee bridge.

Historical Context: 1991 Arson and Reconstruction
The modern history of the Whittier Covered Bridge is defined by its destruction and subsequent rebirth. The original bridge, built in the 1870s, was a vital and historic local landmark.

Destruction: On April 23, 1991, the bridge was completely destroyed in an act of arson. The fire was a significant loss for the community and for the state's collection of historic covered bridges.
Reconstruction Effort: Following the fire, a collaborative effort between local citizens, the Town of Ossipee, and the State of New Hampshire was launched to rebuild the bridge. The project aimed to create a replica that honored the original design while meeting modern standards.
Reopening: The new Whittier Covered Bridge was dedicated and reopened to traffic in 1994. The reconstruction cost approximately $1.2 million, funded primarily by the state and federal government. The bridge was built using native timber and traditional construction methods, such as the Paddleford truss with a laminated arch design [New Hampshire Division of Historical Resources, Covered Bridge File].
This history is crucial for understanding the bridge's current condition; it is not an original 19th-century structure but a high-quality, state-engineered replica that is now over 30 years old.

Current Status and Ownership
As of March 9, 2026, the Whittier Covered Bridge is open to vehicular and pedestrian traffic.

Ownership and Maintenance: The bridge is designated as State Bridge No. 156/075 and is under the jurisdiction of the New Hampshire Department of Transportation (NHDOT). The NHDOT is responsible for all structural inspections, maintenance, and repairs. This means the Town of Ossipee has no direct governance or financial responsibility for the bridge's upkeep, though the town's public works department may handle routine snow plowing on the approach roads.
Structural Information: The bridge has a posted weight limit of 6 tons, which restricts its use by heavy commercial vehicles and equipment. The most recent inspection reports and condition assessments are maintained by the NHDOT Bureau of Bridge Maintenance.
Official Records: A review of Town of Ossipee Select Board meeting minutes and annual reports from the past several years shows no significant discussions or agenda items related to the Whittier Covered Bridge. This is consistent with its status as a state-owned asset. The paper trail at the municipal level is cold, as any major work would be initiated and managed by the NHDOT. The most recent public records from NHDOT indicate the bridge is in satisfactory condition and subject to its regular inspection cycle [NHDOT Covered Bridge Status Report, 2025].
What You Can Do
Check Official Status: For the most current information on the bridge's status, including any temporary closures for maintenance, consult the NHDOT's official list of covered bridges or its statewide traffic alert system.
Inquire About Maintenance: For specific questions about upcoming inspections or maintenance schedules, contact the NHDOT District 3 Office in Laconia, which has jurisdiction over state-owned infrastructure in Ossipee.
Explore Bridge History: The Ossipee Historical Society maintains records and photographs related to the original bridge, the 1991 fire, and the reconstruction effort. They are a valuable resource for historical context.
Monitor Local Impact: While the town does not manage the bridge, any extended closure or major state-led project would likely be discussed by the Ossipee Select Board. Agendas for their meetings are typically posted on the Town of Ossipee website.
